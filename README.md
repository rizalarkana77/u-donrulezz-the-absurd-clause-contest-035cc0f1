## Entry

Section 8.3: The Company may, at its sole discretion, replace the User's morning alarm with a small brass band stationed outside the bedroom window, effective immediately and without compensation.

## Why this wins

This clause is engineered to ambush. It opens with pure terms-of-service boilerplate — a section number, "The Company may, at its sole discretion" — the exact phrasing that makes eyes glaze over in a real agreement. Only then does the absurdity arrive, as a single concrete image: not a vague penalty, but a small brass band physically stationed outside the bedroom window. The closer, "effective immediately and without compensation," snaps straight back into deadpan legal register, so the joke comes from the register itself rather than any winking or explanation. It is one tight sentence, original and specific, funny on a single reading with no outside setup, and it could genuinely sit buried at Section 8.3 of a 40-page agreement without anyone noticing until 6 a.m.

## Run

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
```

This writes the entry line (no trailing newline) to `out/entry.txt`, verifies it by reading it back, and prints its SHA-256 hash. The program uses only the Node standard library and is deterministic: repeated runs produce byte-identical output.
